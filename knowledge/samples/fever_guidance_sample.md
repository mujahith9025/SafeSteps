# Adult Fever Triage and Care

## Understanding Body Temperature and Fever
A normal adult body temperature typically fluctuates between 36.1°C and 37.2°C (97°F to 99°F) throughout the day, influenced by physical activity, hormonal cycles, and circadian rhythm. A clinical fever is defined as a core body temperature rising to 38.0°C (100.4°F) or above. Fever is not a disease in itself, but rather an adaptive immune mechanism through which the human body mobilizes white blood cells and creates an inhospitable thermal environment for invading pathogens, such as viral respiratory infections or bacterial colonies.

## Home Supportive Care and Safe Management
For mild to moderate fevers where red-flag symptoms are absent, supportive home care focuses on maintaining patient comfort and preventing fluid deficits:
- Regular fluid replacement: High body temperatures accelerate insensible fluid loss through perspiration and rapid respiration. Individuals should consume frequent sips of clean water, oral rehydration solutions, or clear vegetable broths.
- Rest and thermoregulation: Avoid excessive layering or heavy blankets, which trap heat and cause rebound hyperthermia. Wear light, breathable cotton clothing and rest in a well-ventilated room.
- Over-the-counter antipyretics: If the fever causes physical discomfort or muscle aches, paracetamol is commonly considered a first-line option. Always verify liver health, avoid exceeding maximum daily limits, and consult a qualified pharmacist or doctor before taking medications.

## When to Seek Medical Evaluation Within 24 to 48 Hours
Not all fevers resolve spontaneously. Adults should arrange a clinical consultation with a primary care doctor or urgent care clinic if:
- The fever persists for more than 72 consecutive hours without a downward trend.
- The temperature repeatedly spikes above 39.0°C (102.2°F) despite cooling and hydration.
- The fever is accompanied by localized symptoms such as a painful productive cough, burning during urination, or worsening localized abdominal discomfort.
- The affected person lives with pre-existing chronic conditions, including diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease, or immunocompromising treatments.

## Critical Red Flags Requiring Emergency Care
Immediate emergency evaluation (calling 112 or visiting the nearest emergency department) is mandatory if a fever occurs alongside:
- Severe neck stiffness where touching the chin to the chest causes excruciating pain.
- Acute altered mental state, confusion, slurred speech, or unexplained drowsiness.
- New onset non-blanching petechial skin rash (a rash that does not fade when pressed firmly with a clear drinking glass).
- Rapid breathing, severe shortness of breath, central chest tightness, or cyanosis (blue discoloration around the lips or fingernails).
