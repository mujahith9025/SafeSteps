# Mild Dehydration and Oral Rehydration Care

## Understanding Fluid Balance and Electrolytes
The human body maintains internal homeostasis through an exact equilibrium of total body water and essential electrolytes, principally sodium, potassium, chloride, and bicarbonate. Fluid loss occurs continuously via sweating, breathing, urine production, and stool elimination. When fluid loss exceeds intake—frequently triggered by high environmental heat, physical exertion, prolonged fever, or gastrointestinal illness such as vomiting and diarrhoea—dehydration develops. Mild dehydration represents an approximate loss of 1% to 3% of body weight in fluid, triggering homeostatic mechanisms including dry mouth, increased thirst, reduced urine volume, and slightly darker urine coloration.

## Principles of Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)
Oral rehydration therapy is one of the most effective, validated primary health interventions worldwide for replenishing water and electrolyte deficits:
- Science of the sodium-glucose cotransporter: The intestinal epithelial lining absorbs water effectively when sodium and glucose are ingested in a balanced equimolar proportion. Pure plain water in large quantities fails to replenish lost minerals and may lead to dilution hyponatremia.
- Standard WHO formulation: Oral rehydration salts sachets should be reconstituted precisely according to manufacturer package directions in clean drinking water (typically one standard sachet dissolved in exactly 1 liter of boiled and cooled water).
- Pacing fluid delivery: Rather than drinking large glasses rapidly—which may trigger gastrointestinal distension and induce vomiting—fluid should be delivered as frequent, small sips (5 to 10 mL every five minutes using a teaspoon or small cup).

## Monitoring Symptoms and Recovery Pathways
During fluid recovery, patients should monitor clinical markers of adequate rehydration:
- Return of light pale yellow urine output within four to six hours.
- Relief of dry mucosal membranes, thirst, and mild fatigue.
- Continued consumption of bland, easily digestible foods such as rice, bananas, oats, and boiled potatoes once nausea abates. Avoid hyperosmolar sweet fruit juices or carbonated sodas, which draw fluid into the intestinal lumen and worsen loose stools.

## Red Flags Indicating Severe Dehydration (Emergency Care)
Immediate emergency medical care (call 112) or urgent intravenous fluid administration is required if any of the following critical indicators appear:
- Complete absence of urination (anuria) for more than eight consecutive hours in adults.
- Severe postural dizziness, fainting (syncope), confusion, or inability to stand upright.
- Sunken eyes, extremely dry parchment-like skin that fails to rebound when pinched (prolonged skin turgor loss), and cold clammy extremities.
- Inability to retain any fluids orally due to unremitting, continuous vomiting.
